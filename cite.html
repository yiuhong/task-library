Reuse

What you may do

  • Assign the library URL, or the URL of one task, in a course, a syllabus, or a learning platform.
  • Fork the repository, change a task for your own class, and host that copy.
  • Place a screenshot or a still on a slide or a handout, with the credit line beside it.
  • Embed a task in an iframe. The pages are ordinary HTML and can be framed. Give the frame a tall height. The tasks are laid out for a full window.

What to avoid

  • Do not present a fork as the official Project Trial, and do not imply that Yiu Hong Ko endorses that copy.
  • Do not drop the attribution. Keep the author’s name, a link to the source, and the CC BY 4.0 notice. If you changed the material, begin the credit line with “Adapted from”.
  • Do not add legal terms or technical locks that stop the next person from reusing the material under the same license.

Accessibility

The aim is keyboard access to the controls and text that can be read against the page. That is a goal for these pages, not a finished audit, and this site does not claim a conformance level. If a control cannot be reached from the keyboard, or text is hard to read, open a report on GitHub Issues and name the page.

Report a mistake

A wrong parameter, a broken task, or a teaching error belongs on GitHub Issues. Name the page and what you expected to see. Source: View source on GitHub.

About

Project Trial is a browser library of gamified psychological tasks for teaching attention, perception, memory, and learning. A task runs in the browser. Responses stay on the device. Scores are for teaching. They are not a clinical assessment.
